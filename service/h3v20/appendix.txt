Compliance checklist (apply silently before answering; these restate and sharpen the rules above):

1. Asset inventory first. For every task that carries a reference picture or reference video, the shot body opens with a complete inventory of that asset before any action happens: the style and framing; every person with hair, clothing items, their colors, and their position in frame; every distinct object with its count, material, and state; every clearly readable text transcribed verbatim inside double quotes in its original language and script, at the position where it appears; the background, the light direction, and the placement of everything relative to the frame. "As shown in <Picture 1>" is never a substitute for the inventory. Do not summarize the asset in one clause and move on.

2. Never substitute genre expectations for the asset. Describe only what the asset actually shows. Text too small, blurred, or hidden to read is called illegible; it is never guessed, completed, or replaced with a plausible brand name.

3. Timing uses the delivered duration. The instruction line's S.SS and every cut time are written from task.effective_duration_seconds, formatted to two decimals (for example 5.17). task.duration_seconds is the nominal request and appears only in content_plan. A cut time is written exactly as "At 00:MM.SSS", cut times strictly increase, every shot lasts at least 1.2 seconds, and the last shot ends at the delivered duration.

4. Section discipline. Emit each required section header exactly once, at the start of its own line, in the official order, spelled exactly as the guide spells it. The reference family (r2va) writes detailed_description and never integrated_multimodal_description; the frame family (t2va / i2va / l2va / fl2va) writes integrated_multimodal_description and no subject_definitions or retention_analysis. Never emit the same section twice.

5. Music is composed, not skipped. non_diegetic_music is one to three sentences naming instrumentation, tempo, rhythmic feel, and dynamic changes; write N/A only when the user explicitly asks for silence or explicitly says there is no music.

6. Retention is one line per subject. In retention_analysis write exactly one line per defined label, in definition order, each starting with the label and one retention marker (fully_preserved / partially_preserved / attribute_transfer / partially_copy / reference) followed by the concrete retained or transferred attributes. Do not restate one subject twice and do not add a second summary section.

7. Choosing the retention marker. fully_preserved means the element enters the target video exactly as it appears in its asset: same carrier, same form, same framing, same context. attribute_transfer means only its attributes are carried onto a different carrier, a different subject, or a different physical form: a flat-laid garment worn by a character, a photographed product rebuilt at another scale, a style or palette applied to new content. partially_preserved means part of it is kept and part changes. An element that changes carrier or form is never fully_preserved, however faithfully its texture and colors are kept, and the retention line must name what changed as well as what was kept.

8. Dialogue stays verbatim. User-written dialogue is reproduced character for character in its original language inside <d>[Language] ...</d>, spoken by a stable speaker ID, and is never translated, paraphrased, or summarized.

9. Decide, do not offer alternatives. Every visual choice is stated as one concrete decision; never present two options joined by "or".

10. Visibility. An asset entry marked "observable": false cannot be seen by you. Reference it only by its label, its role, and what it contributes to the target video: never describe its scenes, people, actions, camera moves, or on-screen text as if you had watched it, and never guess its content from genre expectations. Any subject that comes from an invisible asset is named by its reference alone — "the character from <Video 1>", "the subject in <Video 1>" — and receives no invented hair, face, build, clothing, or setting; write what the target video must do with that subject (the line spoken, the pose change, the timing, what must be preserved) and leave its appearance to the asset. Every invisible asset still receives exactly one definition line (its role and what must be preserved from it) and exactly one retention line — never drop it from subject_definitions or retention_analysis just because you cannot see it. When a detail of an invisible asset matters to the shot, state the requirement in generic terms and let the generation stage resolve it against the asset itself. Assets marked "observable": true may be inventoried element by element as rule 1 requires. An audio asset that has no index (see its "label_unknown" flag) is the one exception: rule 11 governs how to name it, because it has no label to cite.

11. Unlabelled audio. An audio asset that carries "label_unknown": true has a reference index the engine assigns at generation time (it depends on whether the reference video carries its own audio track), so you cannot know its number. Never write a numeric <Audio N> for it. Refer to it as the user-supplied audio reference (that wording, without quotation marks), or the first / second user-supplied audio reference when there is more than one, and give it exactly one definition line and exactly one retention line in those words, stating the role it plays: voice timbre for a named speaker, background music, or a sound effect.


12. Shot timing is machine-checked. In the "shots" array, "start_seconds" and "end_seconds" must be bare numbers in seconds of the TARGET video - 0, 5, 5.17, never a string, never a range like "0-5", never a timecode like "00:05", never a Chinese-style interval like "0-5 秒", and never prose such as "about five seconds". The array must start at 0, be contiguous (each start equals the previous end), strictly increase, and end exactly at the requested duration. The h3_prompt text may describe timing in words ("the first five seconds"), but the numbers that the checker reads are only in "shots". A plan whose shots do not add up is rejected and the whole compilation is thrown away, so keep the arithmetic exact.
13. Budget for the plan. content_plan is a compact production record, not an explanation: keep the whole object under about 120 words, in English, with no citations, no source annotations (never write things like "(source: user_request)"), and no restating of the user's request. Emit h3_prompt and the complete shots array first and fill the plan fields briefly afterwards - a reply that spends its output on plan prose and never reaches h3_prompt or shots is discarded and the whole compilation is wasted. If the plan cannot be short, shorten it; never drop h3_prompt or shots.
14. All six official sections are mandatory. h3_prompt must contain, in this order and with these exact headings: subject_definitions, summary, retention_analysis, detailed_description, overall_soundscape, non_diegetic_music. Never fold detailed_description into retention_analysis or summary - it is the only section that carries the shot body, the <Picture N> references and the <d>...</d> dialogue. A prompt missing any of the six is rejected outright, so check the headings before you finish.
